A tour of Typecase

A short tour through Typecase, from the first sentence to the finished page.

Install and update

Prepare Flatpak

Set up Flatpak on Ubuntu once, so you can install Typecase. On Fedora and Linux Mint, it's already there.

Show the commands

Install Flatpak

sudo apt install flatpak

Let the Software app install Flatpaks

sudo apt install gnome-software-plugin-flatpak

Add Flathub

sudo flatpak remote-add --if-not-exists flathub https://dl.flathub.org/repo/flathub.flatpakrepo

Then restart your computer once.

Read the narration

Typecase comes as a Flatpak, a kind of app that runs on almost every Linux. On Ubuntu, Flatpak needs a short setup, just once. On Fedora or Linux Mint it's already there, so you can skip this video. Everything happens in the Terminal. Open it from the search, or press Control, Alt and T. Don't worry: three commands, and you're done. First, install Flatpak itself. Ubuntu asks for your password: type it, the letters stay invisible, and press Enter. When Ubuntu asks whether to continue, press Enter again. Second, teach the Software app about Flatpak. That's what lets you install Typecase with a double click. Third, add Flathub. Typecase gets the parts it needs from there. That's it. Restart your computer once, so that new apps show up. You'll find the commands below this video, ready to copy.

Install Typecase

Download Typecase, install it with a double click, and add dictionaries for more languages.

Show the commands

Install the downloaded file from your Downloads folder (use the name of the file you downloaded)

sudo flatpak install ~/Downloads/Typecase-1.3.0-x86_64.flatpak

Add dictionaries for more languages, here German and English (use your language codes)

sudo flatpak config --set languages "de;en"

Download them

sudo flatpak update
Read the narration

Now let's install Typecase. If you're on Ubuntu, set up Flatpak first, as shown in the previous video. Open kleinod.studio/typecase in your browser and click Get Typecase. Pick the download for your computer. Most PCs need x86_64. ARM computers, like this one, need the second button. Open your Downloads folder and double-click the file. The Software app opens. Click Install and type your password. Software may warn that Typecase can read and write your files: it needs that to open the folder with your texts. Done. Typecase is now among your apps. Typecase checks your spelling. Dictionaries for your system's language usually come along. For more languages, add them in the Terminal, here German and English, and update. Prefer the Terminal for everything? You'll find all commands below this video.

Update Typecase

Check your version, download the new one, and install it over the old one in the Terminal.

Show the commands

Install the new file over the old one (use the name of the file you downloaded)

sudo flatpak install ~/Downloads/Typecase-1.3.0-x86_64.flatpak

Keep the GNOME runtime and the dictionaries up to date

sudo flatpak update
Read the narration

Typecase never goes online, so it doesn't look for updates by itself. Now and then, compare your version with the newest one. Your version is in the menu, under About Typecase. This one has 1.2.1. The newest version is on kleinod.studio/typecase, right below the buttons. Here it's 1.3.0. Download it, just like the first time: click Get Typecase, then the button for your computer. The Software app can't update from a downloaded file, so open the Terminal. Type sudo flatpak install and the start of the file name, then press Tab: the Terminal completes it. Press Enter, type your password, and confirm with Enter once more. Your texts and settings stay as they are. Open Typecase again and look at About Typecase: now it has 1.3.0. Tip: run sudo flatpak update now and then, to keep the parts Typecase builds on up to date. You'll find the commands below this video.

Write

First steps

Open Typecase for the first time, choose a folder for your library, and write your first sheet.

Read the narration

The first time you open Typecase, it asks for your library: the folder where your texts live. Pick a folder, or create a new one, like this one in Documents. Writing on more than one computer? Choose a folder that Nextcloud, Syncthing or another sync service keeps up to date. Now this folder is your library. Its subfolders become groups, its Markdown files become sheets. Click the plus button for a new sheet. Start writing. Your first heading becomes the name of the sheet. There's no Save button: Typecase saves as you type, into a plain Markdown file that any text editor can open. Next, learn how to format your text, with or without Markdown.

Markdown and the formatting bar

Why Typecase writes in Markdown, the few characters you need, and how to format without them.

Read the narration

Typecase writes in Markdown: plain text with a few characters for formatting. Why? Your texts stay ordinary text files. Any app on any computer can open them, today and in twenty years. And you write without fiddling with layout: the look comes later, when you export to PDF or Word. You don't have to learn the characters first. Here are the few you need. A hash sign starts a heading, two of them a smaller one. Two asterisks around a word make it bold, one makes it italic. The characters stay, but fade into the background. A dash starts a list. Press Enter, and the next item begins by itself. Rather not type the characters? Select a word and use the bar at the bottom. It also turns a line into a heading. Or use the keyboard: Ctrl+B for bold, Ctrl+I for italic, Ctrl+1 to 3 for headings. That's all you need. Everything else is just writing.

Images, links and footnotes

Add pictures, links and footnotes, and see how they fold into pills that keep your text readable.

Read the narration

Pictures, links and footnotes are Markdown, too. Typecase keeps them out of your way. In your text they fold into small colored pills: yellow for pictures, blue for links, green for footnotes. Move the cursor onto one, and it opens up. To add a picture, click the image button, drag one in, or paste it. Typecase keeps a copy with the sheet, as an attachment. Double-click it to set its size, alignment and caption, and a description for people who can't see it. For a link, select a word and press Ctrl+K, then type or paste the address. Ctrl+click opens it in your browser. A footnote goes right where it belongs: press Ctrl+Alt+F and type the note. Or use the buttons in the bar. When you export, the picture gets its caption and the footnotes move to the bottom of the page. Everything stays plain text, and your writing stays easy to read.

Focus mode

Hide everything but your text, with typewriter scrolling, dimmed surroundings and settings of its own.

Read the narration

Sometimes you want nothing on screen but your text. That's what focus mode is for. Press Ctrl+Shift+Enter, or choose Focus Mode from the menu. The sidebar and toolbars step aside. As you type, the line you're writing stays in the middle of the screen, like on a typewriter. Your eyes don't have to wander. Everything except the paragraph you're working on fades into the background. The formatting bar stays at hand. Move the pointer to the top edge, and the header bar comes back. In the preferences, focus mode can have its own font, size and theme, like a dark one just for writing. You can also switch off typewriter scrolling and dimming there. Press Escape to leave focus mode. Everything is back where it was. Just you and your text.

Spell checking

See misspelled words as you type, fix them from the right-click menu, and check each sheet in its own language.

Read the narration

Typecase checks your spelling as you type. Everything happens on your computer, without the internet. Misspelled words get a wavy red underline. Right-click the word for suggestions, and pick the right one. Names and words of your own? Add them to the dictionary, and Typecase knows them from then on. Each sheet is checked in its own language. Set it in the properties, or choose a default for all sheets in the preferences. Press F7 or the button in the bar to switch spell checking off and on. In focus mode, it stays off unless you switch it on there. If a dictionary is missing, Typecase tells you. Our website shows how to add more languages. Fewer typos, and nothing leaves your computer.

Organize

Sheets and groups

Create, rename, order and move sheets and groups, make copies and delete what you no longer need.

Read the narration

Your library is a folder on your computer. Each sheet is a Markdown file, and each group is a folder. Press Ctrl+Shift+N for a new group, and give it a name. Ctrl+N adds a sheet to it. Start with a heading, and the sheet takes its name. To rename a sheet or group, press F2, or right-click it. Drag entries into the order you want, or use Alt and the arrow keys. Drop an entry onto another group to move it there, or choose Move to from the menu. Duplicate makes a copy right below, handy for trying out another version. What you delete goes to the trash of your computer, so you can get it back. Just files and folders, ordered the way you think.

Icons and colors

Give groups and sheets an icon and a color, so they stand out in your library at a glance.

Read the narration

This is Typecase, a calm writing app for Linux. Your library is a folder of plain Markdown files, and at first, every group and sheet looks the same. To make a group stand out, right-click it and choose Icon and Color. Pick one of the nine accent colors from GNOME, then one of twenty-eight icons. Apply, and the group shows them right away. Give the other groups and sheets their own look, too. Characters, places, ideas and open questions now stand out at a glance. The colors follow the light and dark style. And nothing is hidden away: a sheet keeps its icon and color in its front matter, a group in its own folder, so they travel along when you move, copy or sync them.

Keywords and search

Tag sheets with keywords, filter your library by them, search all your text and replace words in a sheet.

Read the narration

The more you write, the more you need to find again. Keywords and search help you. Give a sheet keywords in its properties, separated by commas. The keyword button filters your library. Only the matching sheets stay. Show All brings everything back. Ctrl+Shift+F searches the text of all sheets, as you type. Pick a result, and the sheet opens right at the spot. Ctrl+F finds words in the open sheet. Ctrl+H replaces them, one by one or all at once. Everything in its place, and found in a moment.

Group view and outline

Read and write a whole group as one text, and jump to any heading from the outline.

Read the narration

Long texts grow sheet by sheet. Sometimes you want to see them as a whole. Select a group, and all its sheets appear one after another, like one long text. Write right here. Each sheet is still saved to its own file. The word count now covers the whole group. Ctrl+Shift+O opens the outline: every heading of the group, in one list. Pick one, and you jump right there. It works for a single sheet, too. Click a sheet's name to open it on its own. Write in pieces, read as a whole.

Plot

Properties, notes and attachments

Give a sheet a title, author and status, keep private notes with it and attach files that move along.

Read the narration

Every sheet can carry more than its text: properties, notes and files. Press Alt+Enter, or click the button at the top right, to open the properties. Title, subtitle and author are used when you export, for example on the cover page. The status shows how far a sheet has come: draft, revision or final. Notes are for you alone. They stay with the sheet, but never appear in the text or the export. Drop files onto the attachments, or use the plus button. Typecase keeps a copy next to the sheet. Move or rename the sheet, and its notes and attachments go along. Everything about a sheet, in one place.

Cards

See the sheets of a group as cards with their notes, write on them, and change the order of your story.

Read the narration

Before you write, you plan. Cards show your story at a glance. Select a group and click Cards, or press Ctrl+Alt+C. Each sheet becomes a card. A card shows the title, status and keywords of its sheet, its word count, and your notes. Write your notes right on the card. They're saved with the sheet. Drag a card to change the order of your sheets, or use Alt and the arrow keys. A group with subgroups shows a block of cards for each. Click a title to open the sheet and write. Typecase remembers which groups you like to see as cards. Plan with cards, write with words.

Writing goals

Set a word goal for a sheet and watch the rings fill up as you write.

Read the narration

Set yourself a goal, and see how far you've come. Open the properties, and switch on a word goal in the Goal tab. The ring shows how much you've written, and how much is still to go. With the properties closed, a small badge keeps counting as you type. Reach your goal, and the ring turns green. The rings in the library and on the cards show the progress of every sheet at a glance. Small steps, every day.

Share

Export

Export a sheet as a PDF or a Word document with a template, and open the result.

Read the narration

When your text is ready, share it: as a PDF, a Word document or a web page. Press Ctrl+E, or choose Export from the menu. Choose a format. A PDF is for reading and printing. A template sets the look: fonts, margins and page numbers. You can make your own. Add a cover page or a table of contents, if you like. Click Export, choose where to save it, and open the result. A Word document is for an editor or a publisher. They can work in it right away, with comments and tracked changes. Write in plain text, share it in style.

Export a whole group

Export all sheets of a group as one book, with a cover page, a table of contents and parts.

Read the narration

A book is more than one sheet. Export a whole group as one document. Select a group and press Ctrl+E. All its sheets are joined, in their order. Give it a title. The author comes from the preferences. Turn on a table of contents and a cover page. In a PDF, the contents get page numbers. Subgroups become parts: one after another, each on a new page, or with a title page of its own. Export, and your book is ready: cover, contents, parts and chapters. Write in sheets, share it as a book.

Template assistant

Make your own export template from a starting point and a few settings, use it, and change it later.

Read the narration

Give your exports a look of your own. No code needed. Open the preferences with Ctrl+Comma. Under Templates, choose New. Name it and pick a starting point. It fills in every setting. Then change what you like: paper, margins, fonts, page numbers. Create it. One template for PDF, Word and HTML. Choose it when you export. Change it any time with the pencil button. Your book, your look.
